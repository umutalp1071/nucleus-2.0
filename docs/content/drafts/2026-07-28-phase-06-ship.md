# Nucleus now writes the plan, including when to quit

A validated idea used to dead-end at a verdict. Now it becomes a plan someone
could actually act on Monday morning -- positioning, first 10 users,
milestones, and a stopping condition.

**Where it stands:** "Advance to Planning" runs two chained AI calls --
`plan-venture` then `scope-mvp` -- and produces a real plan artifact and an
MVP scope artifact. Every plan has a required `killCriteria` field. Every
scope has a required `explicitlyNot` list with at least 3 real cuts, rendered
struck-through so they read as "deliberately removed," not "more features."

**The problem:** every AI planner I've tried adds features. None of them are
graded on what they remove.

**How it works now:**

```ts
explicitlyNot: z.array(z.string()).min(3),  // the cuts are the scope
milestones: z.array(z.object({
  name: z.string(), outcome: z.string(), estimateDays: z.number().max(14),
})).min(3).max(6),
```

No milestone over 14 days -- if it would take longer, it isn't a milestone,
it's a project pretending to be one.

**The call I made:** if the second call (scoping the MVP) fails after the
first (the plan) already succeeded, the plan stays saved and the venture
stays at "validated" rather than losing everything. Partial progress the
user already paid for is never discarded.

**Next:** the build stage -- milestones become checkable tasks, and Nucleus
generates a handoff spec for whatever tool actually writes the code.
